# Personal homepage hierarchy

Approved by Hao in conversation on 2026-10-31.

## Intent

Lead with Hao's research identity in self-evolving agentic systems, harnesses,
and context infrastructure, without making the homepage an AutoIA landing page.
Retain database, graph, AI-powered data processing, and hardware acceleration
as independent research contributions.

## Chosen approach

Keep the existing editorial theme, hero title, personal photograph, page URLs,
and news feed. Replace the long project-led introduction with a short personal
introduction. Show four research directions in this order: Agentic Infrastructure,
AI-Powered Data Systems, Database & Graph Systems, Hardware-Accelerated Computing.
Agent memory belongs under context infrastructure; vector and graph databases
belong under Database & Graph Systems.

AutoIA gets a distinct Current Focus section after Research Directions. The
Selected Systems section then contains only SEMA, GES, and TQEX. Retain scoped
performance evidence but remove the baseline count from the SEMA homepage copy.
Add the Chinese explanation 个人金牌 to the Huawei individual award. Replace the
collaboration paragraph with an open invitation to exchange ideas, discuss
research, and explore collaborations, followed by internship contact details.

The earlier alternatives were a project-led landing page and four equally
prominent identities in the hero. Neither captured the intended balance: one
clear current research identity, backed by a broad independent track record.

## Layout and acceptance

Use two columns for the research directions on desktop and one on mobile.
Keep the existing section-label/content layout and theme variables. Use short
system headings and paragraphs rather than dense inline bullet descriptions.
Keep project detail pages, paper attribution, and benchmark source links intact.
Update source and built-site contracts for the new hierarchy, three selected
systems, topic placement, responsive grid, contact wording, and award label.
Require successful build, contract tests, internal-link checks, and deployment;
check the live homepage separately rather than treating a Git commit as release
verification.
